# Naming research, round 5: people and crafts that manage branches

Round 5, 2026-10-11. Availability came from `namecheck.sh` (read-only lookups). A 404 means free. A 200 means taken. A 000 means the lookup timed out, so the result is unverified. I registered nothing anywhere.

Limits of this round, stated up front:
- Only 10 names went through the availability script.
- Only 3 web searches were run: Pointsman as a software name, the ditch rider job, and the lengthsman job.
- Every other domain below was covered from memory, without a web search. Names that came from those domains are listed for the record and were not checked.

## 1. Domains searched
- Railways: points, signal box, marshalling yard, shunting.
- Irrigation and water-sharing: the ditch rider (US West), the zanjero (Los Angeles), the acequia mayordomo.
- Canals: the lengthsman, the lock keeper.
- Hedges and orchards: hedge laying. Pruning and espalier were already used in earlier rounds.
- Interchanges, braiding, coral and lightning: brainstormed only. No checked candidate came out of them.

## 2. Finalists

### Pointsman
- **What it is:** the railway worker who sets the points (switches). Setting the points decides which branch a train takes. Not web-checked as a definition.
- **Fit:** he sends traffic onto branches and brings branches back into the main line. That is what checkout and merge do. The hidden meaning is clear, and the word sounds professional.
- **Command:** `pm` or `pts`. Not checked on PATH. `pm` may clash with other tools; this is a question, not checked.
- **Checks:**
  - Registries: crates 404, brew 404, cask 404, npm 404, PATH free.
  - GitHub: 45 repos. The top ones are mkgor/pointsman (3★, a router), im9/pointsman (3★) and pooriaanv/Pointsman (1★).
  - Web: no git tool found (search linked in Sources).

### Ditchrider
- **What it is:** an irrigation district worker. He works the headgates and turnouts that send water into each lateral, and he shares the water fairly ([COID](https://coid.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Patrolman-Ditchrider.pdf), [Tumalo](https://tumalo.specialdistrict.org/files/a2e282c95/Ditch+Rider_Job+Description.pdf)).
- **Fit:** he watches over a network of branches and patrols it for breaks. The meaning holds. The sound is rustic and may not read as a git tool quickly.
- **Command:** `dr`. Not checked.
- **Checks:**
  - Registries: all 404, PATH free.
  - GitHub: 0 repos.
  - Web: only the job postings. No product search was run.

### Lengthsman
- **What it is:** the worker who looks after one length of canal, railway or road ([Warwickshire](https://www.warwickshire.gov.uk/community/lengthsman-scheme), [Basingstoke Canal](https://basingstoke-canal.org.uk/?p=1489)).
- **Fit:** weak. He looks after a stretch, not branches.
- **Command:** `lm`. Not checked.
- **Checks:**
  - Registries: all 404, PATH free.
  - GitHub: 0 repos.
  - Web: no product search was run.

### Zanjero
- **What it is:** the historic water-ditch overseer of Los Angeles. This comes from memory, without a source.
- **Fit:** the meaning is good. The foreign word fails the test of reading as a git tool quickly.
- **Command:** `zj`. Not checked.
- **Checks:**
  - Registries: all 404, PATH free.
  - GitHub: 3 repos, all 0★.
  - Web: no product search was run.

### Lockkeeper
- **What it is:** the person who works a canal lock. Not web-checked.
- **Fit:** a lock lets boats between levels. That is linear, not branching, so the fit is weak.
- **Command:** `lk`. Not checked.
- **Checks:**
  - Registries: all 404, PATH free.
  - GitHub: Hannay001/lockkeeper (17★, an AI skill router).
  - Web: no product search was run.

## 4. Top 3 compared with Graftwright
1. **Pointsman.** This is the only one that rivals Graftwright. It is a real trade whose whole job is choosing branches, and it is clean everywhere. Against it: it reads "railway" before "git", and a "-man" name is less brandable than "-wright". Graftwright still reads as git faster.
2. **Ditchrider.** It is completely free and the meaning is accurate. It sounds rustic, so it is weaker than Graftwright on "professional".
3. **Lengthsman.** It is free and sounds professional, but the branch meaning is weak. It does not beat Graftwright.

Open question for the caller: is Pointsman's railway reading acceptable to the user? I could not confirm this. It is his taste call.
